\chapter*{Voltage Angle Step Test on Grid Forming Converters}
\section*{Test case description}
This test case implements a scenario from the I18 technical documentation of the French Grid (DTR Chapter 8, Article 8.3.3). The chosen test case is named \texttt{S\_VolAngStep7\_OC4} and applied identically to three Grid-Forming
converter architectures:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{DynGFMVSM} — Virtual Synchronous Machine control with a PLL feeding the
      Control block frequency reference; the Converter's Park frame remains
      slaved to the internal virtual angle produced by the swing equation.
\item \texttt{DynGFMVSMConvPLL} — same as above, with the grid-measured PLL frequency
      (\texttt{omegaPLL}) additionally fed to the converter block for frame alignment. The reason behind this variation is to see how mirroring the grid frequency in the Converter's loop will affect performance.
\item \texttt{DynGFMDroop} — Droop-based architecture, with no PLL in the active control chain.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Network and operating point}
The converter is connected to an infinite bus (named Grid) through a static RL line sized for
$SCR_{min} = 2$ (i.e. $x_{grid} = 0.5$~p.u. on the converter's own $S_{Nom}$ base, with $x_{eff} = 0.27$~p.u.).
Operating point OC4: $P_{ref} = 0.95$ p.u., $Q_{ref} = 0$~p.u., $U_{ref}= 1$~p.u.
All system services (voltage control, frequency regulation, LFSM-U/O) are disabled,
according to section 6, so that only the Grid-Forming voltage-source behaviour is
characterized. A reference circuit is shown in Figure \ref{fig:reference_network}

\paragraph{Event and simulation}
At $t = 10$s, a phase jump $\Delta\theta_{grid} = 26.47 °$ is applied to the infinite
bus voltage representing the grid. Different simulations will be carried out with diverse solvers and timesteps. Common features include a simulation from 0 to 25 seconds, with an event at $t=10$s. The output variable of interest is the active current $I_p$ measured at the Point of Generating Unit (PGU).

\paragraph{Conformity criteria}
The I18 technical documentation enforces regulation on the active current $I_p$ mainly through a set of conformity envelopes and a damping at least equal to 40\% for every oscillation in $I_p$ between 0.1 and 20 Hz. This latter criterion is not covered in the
following results.
\subsection*{Simulation setup}
The simulation is carried out on Dynawo using the IDA Solver.
Since the test case is trivial, there is no need for a specific \textit{.iidm} file, corresponding \textit{.par, .dyd and .jobs} files can be found in the \texttt{/SMIB} directory. Initial variables of active/reactive power and module and phase of voltage are given
as parameters. They are calculated in a separate case identical to the reference network but with no perturbation ($\Delta\theta_{grid}=0$).

\chapter*{Loss of synchronism of a current-limited VSM during a frequency excursion}
\section*{Test case description}
This test case, located in the \texttt{/SMIB/DynGFMCCVSMWindUp} directory, illustrates a known limitation of a Virtual Synchronous Machine whose current is limited by a current saturation: when the current saturates, the VSM loop winds up and the converter can lose synchronism with the grid (pole slipping). It reproduces in Dynawo the Modelica example \texttt{Dynawo.\allowbreak Examples.\allowbreak GridForming.\allowbreak VSM.\allowbreak DynGFMCCVSMSmib}.

\paragraph{Converter and control}
The converter is the current-controlled Grid-Forming converter \texttt{DynGFMCCVSM} (preassembled model \texttt{DynGridFormingCurrent\allowbreak ControlledVirtualSynchronousMachine}, $S_{Nom} = 1000$~MW). Its outer loop is a VSM with an additional angle-damping term:
\begin{align*}
2H \frac{d\omega}{dt} &= P_{FilterRef} - P_{Filter} - k_{VSM}\,(\omega - \omega_{PLL}) \\
\theta &= \omega_{Nom} \left[ \int (\omega - \omega_{Ref})\,dt + K_{DampingAngle}\,(\omega - \omega_{Ref}) \right]
\end{align*}
with $H = 5$~s, $k_{VSM} = 150$ and $K_{DampingAngle} = 0.00318$~s. The angle $\theta$ is thus obtained through a PI action on the frequency error: the proportional term $K_{DampingAngle}$ vanishes in steady state but adds damping to the electromechanical oscillations.

The voltage reference produced by the VSM is converted into a current reference, which goes through a \textbf{current saturation} block before the current loop:
\begin{itemize}
\item the saturation is activated as soon as the module of the current reference or of the current measured at the PCC exceeds $I_{Max} + 0.01$~p.u., with $I_{Max} = 1.2$~p.u., and deactivated when the module of the reference goes back below $I_{Max} - 0.01$~p.u. (hysteresis);
\item when active, the module of the current reference is limited to $I_{Max}$ while its angle in the $dq$ frame is kept, i.e. the ratio between active and reactive currents (P/Q ratio) is preserved.
\end{itemize}
The virtual impedance of the converter is not active in this test ($I_{MaxVI} = 2$~p.u. $> I_{Max}$): the current limitation only relies on the current saturation, and no anti-windup acts on the VSM loop.

\paragraph{Network and operating point}
The converter is connected to a grid voltage source (\texttt{AcGridRoCoF}) through a static RL line ($R = 0.005$~p.u., $X = 0.05$~p.u. on the $S_{Ref} = 100$~MVA base, i.e. $x_{grid} = 0.5$~p.u. on the converter's $S_{Nom}$ base, $SCR = 2$). Operating point: $P_{FilterRef} = -0.95$~p.u. (generator convention: the converter absorbs 950~MW), $Q_{FilterRef} = 0$~p.u., $U_{FilterRef} = 1$~p.u.

\paragraph{Event and simulation}
The grid frequency follows a V-shaped profile (\texttt{AcGridRoCoF} with \texttt{ReturnRoCoF = true}), shown in Figure~\ref{fig:windup_omega}: it decreases with a rate of $-0.05$~p.u./s between $t = 5$~s and $t = 8$~s (down to 0.85~p.u.), then returns to 1~p.u. with a rate of $+0.05$~p.u./s between $t = 8$~s and $t = 11$~s, and is held at 1~p.u. afterwards. The simulation runs from 0 to 25~s with the simplified solver.

The response can be split into three phases:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Frequency decrease} ($5 < t < 8$~s): the VSM follows the grid frequency, the angle $\Delta\theta$ remains small (below 0.65~rad) and the current stays below its limit.
\item \textbf{Frequency increase} ($8 < t < 11$~s): because of its virtual inertia, the converter lags behind the grid (at $t = 10.5$~s, $\omega = 0.958$~p.u. while the grid is at 0.975~p.u.). $\Delta\theta$ becomes strongly negative and the current reaches its limit $I_{Max} = 1.2$~p.u. Once the current is saturated, the active power no longer increases with the angle, so nothing pulls the converter back towards the grid: $\Delta\theta$ crosses $-\pi$ at $t \approx 10.1$~s (first pole slip).
\item \textbf{Grid back at 1~p.u.} ($t > 11$~s): the converter never resynchronizes. $\omega$ remains below 1~p.u. (between 0.975 and 0.9998~p.u., i.e. an average deficit of about 0.003~p.u.) and $\Delta\theta$ keeps decreasing, down to $-20.4$~rad at $t = 25$~s. It crosses $-3\pi$ and $-5\pi$ at $t \approx 13.4$~s and $t \approx 19.9$~s: the converter slips one pole about every 6.6~s. At each slip, the active power swings between $-1.05$~p.u. and $+1.13$~p.u., and the current is pinned at 1.2~p.u. most of the time.
\end{itemize}

This behaviour is a wind-up of the VSM loop: while the current is saturated, the measured power $P_{Filter}$ is limited, so the swing equation integrates a permanent power error and the integral part of $\theta$ drifts without bound. The angle-damping term $K_{DampingAngle}$ adds damping around an equilibrium but cannot prevent this drift. This test case is therefore meant as a reference to assess anti-windup strategies on the VSM loop (e.g. back-calculation of the power lost in the saturation), which are not implemented yet.

\chapter*{Grid Forming Converters with a Plant Controller}
\section*{Test case description}
These test cases, located in the \texttt{/PlantControl} directory, check the behaviour of a Grid-Forming power plant, i.e. a Grid-Forming converter driven by a generic plant controller, when the grid frequency varies.
They feature two different architectures for the Grid-Forming converter:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{VSM} — Virtual Synchronous Machine converter (preassembled model \texttt{DynGridFormingVSM\allowbreak PlantControlFull});
\item \texttt{Droop} — Droop-based converter (preassembled model \texttt{DynGridFormingDroop\allowbreak PlantControlFull}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Network and operating point}
The plant is connected to a grid voltage source (\texttt{AcGridRoCoF}, $1\angle 0$~p.u.) through a static RL line (\texttt{Line1}, $R = 0.0005$~p.u., $X = 0.005$~p.u. on the $S_{Ref} = 100$~MVA base). The measurements are taken at the Point of Common Coupling (PCC), between this line and the plant. Inside the preassembled model, the plant consists of a plant transformer and a plant line between the PCC and the tested Grid-Forming converter. The tested network is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:plant_control_network}.

The two architectures behave very differently:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{VSM}: during the frequency ramp, the virtual inertia of the converter reduces the injected power (down to 0.84~p.u.), which counteracts the frequency rise. When the ramp stops at $t = 8$~s, the power swings above its initial value (up to 1.08~p.u.), then the active power loop of the plant controller slowly brings it back to its reference: at $t = 25$~s, the injected power is 0.990~p.u., for an initial value of 0.990~p.u. Since the plant frequency droop is disabled ($K_{droop} = 0$) and the VSM has no permanent frequency droop, the frequency change has no lasting effect on the injected power.
\item \texttt{Droop}: the injected power decreases almost linearly with the frequency during the ramp, following the converter droop characteristic ($\Delta P = \Delta\omega / m_p \approx 0.03 / 0.013 \approx 2.3$~p.u.), and the plant ends up absorbing 1.13~p.u. from $t \approx 8$~s onwards.
The plant controller cannot restore the initial injection: compensating the converter droop would require a power reference of about 3.2~p.u., well above the limit $P_{Max} = 1.2$~p.u.  on the $S_{Nom}$ base. The final power is consistent with this saturated reference: $1.2 - 2.3 \approx -1.1$~p.u.
\end{itemize}
